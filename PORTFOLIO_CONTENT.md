4. CASE STUDIES
Cammi — AI Marketing Platform · AWS Serverless
One-liner: An AI platform that turns a blank page into a complete marketing campaign.
The Problem: Building marketing foundations by hand is slow. A go-to-market strategy, ICP, or messaging framework can take a team hours to write from scratch — and the downstream content is worse: a single case study or landing page can eat the better part of a day, and a full set of blogs, one-pagers, and campaign assets can stretch across days of back-and-forth. Then it all lives in one tool, gets published in another, and tracked in a third. Cammi compresses that work from hours and days into minutes, in one place.
What I Built: A platform where users generate complete marketing assets — strategy documents, blogs, landing pages, one-pagers, case studies, social posts, and full campaigns — guided by an AI assistant that understands their brand. It connects directly to six tools businesses already use, so content doesn't just get created — it gets published to WordPress and LinkedIn, exported to Google Drive, pushed through HubSpot, and tracked in GA4, without leaving the app.
The Hard Part: I worked across the full stack: the AI document-generation engine on AWS Bedrock, a context-aware chat assistant grounded in each user's own knowledge base using vector search, and the integration layer wiring six external platforms into one product. The whole thing runs serverless, designed to scale without re-architecting.
Stack: Next.js · TypeScript · Material UI · Redux · RTK Query · AWS (Lambda, Step Functions, Bedrock, DynamoDB, S3, API Gateway, EventBridge, Amplify) · WebSockets · S3 Vector Knowledge Base

Inventory & Approval System — Internal Tool · Workflow Automation
One-liner: A request-and-approval system serving 150+ users that replaced spreadsheets and email chains with one source of truth.
The Problem: Tracking who has which laptop, who requested what, and who still needs to approve it usually lives in scattered spreadsheets and long email threads. At ~150 users across four distinct roles, things get lost, approvals stall, and nobody knows the real state of inventory.
What I Built: A role-based platform serving 150+ users across four roles — employees, PMOs, HR, and admins. Employees request equipment; each request routes automatically through the right approval chain (PMO → HR → Admin); and admins manage the full inventory lifecycle, from intake to assignment. Everyone sees exactly what they're permitted to — and nothing slips through the cracks.
The Hard Part: The multi-role permission system and approval-routing logic — the part that's easy to get subtly wrong and frustrating for users when you do. Built the backend in Django and a clean, responsive interface in Next.js.
Stack: Django · Next.js · TypeScript · Material UI
